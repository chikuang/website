Places to share research, meet collaborators, and follow developments in statistics, biostatistics, bioinformatics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence around Atlanta. The directory includes upcoming research conferences, applied workshops, student presentations, and ongoing seminar series, with health-related events covering clinical research, epidemiology, biomedical imaging, and biomolecular design.

Upcoming research conferences and workshops

2026

  • IEEE International Workshop on Machine Learning for Signal Processing (MLSP) — September 28–October 1, 2026 · Georgia State University, Atlanta. Statistical learning and signal processing, including generative models, multimodal learning, and biomedical applications. Venue: Centennial Hall.
  • Georgia Statistics Day — October 5, 2026 · Georgia Tech, Atlanta. A regional statistics meeting with research talks and student posters, connecting statistics with applications across disciplines.
  • Emory AI for Health Symposium — December 4–5, 2026 · Emory University, Atlanta. The fourth symposium on AI in medicine and health, at 1760 Haygood Drive NE.
  • NeurIPS 2026 — Atlanta satellite — December 9–13, 2026 · Georgia World Congress Center, Atlanta. Main-conference sessions and tutorials run December 9–11; workshops follow December 12–13. See the venue information and the official list of 26 Atlanta workshops. Topics include uncertainty-aware decisions, evaluation of agents, reliable clinical simulation, and biomolecular design. Sydney hosts the main meeting December 6–12, while Atlanta and Paris have their own local programs.
    • World Models for High-Stakes Health — December 12. Clinical-trial simulation, treatment-effect estimation, causal inference, and uncertainty quantification using longitudinal patient data; rooms C208–C209.
    • Agentic Intelligence for Medical Imaging and Multimodal Clinical Data (AIM-NeurIPS) — December 12. AI agents that combine medical images, clinical records, and analysis tools, with an emphasis on evaluation, reliability, and human oversight.
    • Generative and Experimental Perspectives for Biomolecular Design (GEM) — December 13. Generative models, active learning, and experimental validation for proteins, molecules, and nucleic acids; relevant to computational biology and drug discovery.
  • Machine Learning & the Physical Sciences — December 11, 2026 · Georgia Tech, Atlanta. Hosted by the AI4Science Center, this independent workshop connects machine learning with physical-science research. It is near the NeurIPS venue but is not an official NeurIPS workshop in 2026.

2027

  • Suddath Symposium — March 3–4, 2027 · Georgia Tech, Atlanta. Experimental, computational, and AI approaches to predictive biomedical models, drug development, and toxicity assessment. Venue: Petit Biotechnology Building, Suddath Seminar Room 1128.
  • APHL ID Lab Con — March 15–18, 2027 · Hyatt Regency Atlanta. An applied public-health laboratory conference with tracks on pathogen genomics, infectious-disease outbreaks, and surveillance. The March 15 Advanced Molecular Detection workshop covers metagenomic sequencing, bioinformatics workflows, and genomic epidemiology. Abstracts are due October 2, 2026.
  • SIAM Conference on Applications of Dynamical Systems (DS27) — May 23–27, 2027 · Hilton Atlanta. Includes data-driven and AI-informed dynamical systems, stochastic dynamics, and scientific applications.
  • ACM Federated Computing Research Conference (FCRC) — June 5–11, 2027 · Hyatt Regency Atlanta. Co-located computing conferences connect statistical modeling, learning theory, simulation, and computing systems. Relevant meetings include:
    • STOC 2027 — June 6–11. Theory of computation, including a day on theoretical computer science and AI.
    • SIGMETRICS 2027 — June 7–11. Measurement, statistical modeling, and performance analysis of computing systems.
    • SIGSIM-PADS 2027 — June 9–11. Simulation methods and systems for modeling complex processes.
    • SPAA and PLDI — parallel algorithms and programming-language research during FCRC week; useful for researchers working on scalable ML and AI systems.
